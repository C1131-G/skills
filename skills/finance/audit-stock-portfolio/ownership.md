# Promoter, insider and institutional activity

Who owns the company and what they are doing with their shares is often the earliest warning. Use the last 8–12 quarters of the shareholding pattern (Screener.in, NSE/BSE filings).

## Promoter holding

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| Holding > 50% and stable | Skin in the game — positive |
| Rising through open-market buys | Strong confidence — positive |
| Falling for 3+ quarters without a stated reason (OFS for a minimum public shareholding rule, funding growth) | Warning — find out why |
| Very low (< 25%) in a promoter-led firm | Weak alignment |
| Professionally managed, no promoter (e.g. widely held) | Judge management and board instead |

## Pledge

| Pledged share of promoter holding | Read |
|---|---|
| 0% | Clean |
| 1–10% | Watch the trend |
| > 10% | Red flag — forced selling risk if price falls |
| Rising pledge | Red flag regardless of level |

## Insider trades (SEBI PIT) and SAST disclosures

- **Cluster buying** by several insiders in the open market is meaningful; a single small buy is not.
- **Insider selling** is weaker evidence (people sell for many reasons) — but large repeated sales by the promoter or CEO need an explanation.
- Ignore ESOP exercises and inter-se promoter transfers; they are not a view on value.

## Institutional holders

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| FII and DII holdings rising over several quarters | Smart money accumulating |
| Mutual-fund count rising | Growing coverage and liquidity |
| Both falling while price is flat or up | Distribution — check why |
| Bulk / block deals | Note buyer and seller; a reputed long-term investor entering is a positive |

## Governance

- Independent directors with real credentials; audit committee chaired by an independent.
- SEBI orders, SFIO or ED cases, or stock-exchange penalties in the past five years.
- Promoter group entities receiving loans, royalties or brand fees.

Any governance failure overrides good numbers: verdict Exit or Avoid.
